# Narrative Weaver: Supporting Reflective Wellbeing in Hybrid Work Through Non-Intrusive AI

**Narrative Weaver** is a human–computer interaction (HCI) project that explores how AI can support employee wellbeing in hybrid work environments through reflection, gentle reminders, and meaningful narratives rather than workplace surveillance or performance evaluation.

The project aims to help employees recognize the value of their daily work, reflect on their emotional wellbeing, and maintain a healthier relationship with their work while preserving personal autonomy and privacy.

## 📌 Project Overview

Hybrid work can contribute to stress, mental fatigue, unclear work priorities, and a feeling of disconnection from organizational goals. Although AI-powered workplace tools can improve productivity, employees may feel uncomfortable when these systems monitor their activities or evaluate their performance.

Narrative Weaver explores a more human-centered approach to workplace AI by providing optional, supportive, and non-intrusive wellbeing interactions.

**Research Question:**

How can AI-generated narratives make employees feel valued and supported without crossing ethical boundaries or becoming intrusive?

## ✨ Key Features

- **Start-of-Day Reflection:** Allows users to reflect on their mood and workload expectations.
- **Midday Wellbeing Reminders:** Provides gentle prompts for taking breaks, drinking water, and stretching.
- **End-of-Day Reflection:** Offers a supportive summary of the day's work and accomplishments.
- **Non-Judgmental Communication:** Uses supportive language rather than evaluative or performance-based messages.
- **User Autonomy:** Keeps interactions optional and emphasizes user control.
- **Privacy and Transparency:** Highlights the importance of clear communication about data use.
- **Meaningful Work Narratives:** Explores ways to connect daily tasks with a broader sense of purpose.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

1. Explore the emotional and wellbeing challenges experienced by employees in hybrid work environments.
2. Understand employee attitudes toward AI-supported workplace wellbeing systems.
3. Design a human-centered AI concept that encourages reflection without intrusive monitoring.
4. Evaluate the usability, comfort, and perceived usefulness of the proposed design.
5. Identify ethical design principles for trustworthy workplace AI.

## 🔬 Research Methodology

The project followed a human-centered design process consisting of formative research, design exploration, and user evaluation.

### 1. Formative Research

- Conducted three semi-structured interviews with participants from industry and academic settings.
- Conducted a two-day diary study with two participants.
- Explored early design ideas through a participatory design activity.

### 2. Data Analysis

Interview responses, diary entries, and prototype feedback were analyzed using a lightweight affinity diagramming approach.

Key themes included:

- Workload pressure and sudden deadlines
- Emotional fatigue and mental wellbeing
- Meaning and recognition at work
- Trust in AI systems
- Employee autonomy and privacy concerns

### 3. Prototype Development

Two prototype stages were explored:

- **Low-Fidelity Prototype:** Investigated interaction timing, notification frequency, and user control.
- **High-Fidelity Prototype:** Refined the interface, interaction flow, and language to make the experience more supportive and less intrusive.

### 4. User Evaluation

A qualitative evaluation was conducted with three participants through semi-structured discussions. Participants reviewed the high-fidelity prototype and provided feedback on usability, comfort, and perceived usefulness.

## 📊 Key Findings

The research identified several important findings:

- Heavy workloads, unclear requirements, and sudden deadlines contribute to workplace stress.
- Meaningful outcomes and positive feedback can improve emotional wellbeing.
- Employees have different levels of comfort with AI-supported reflection.
- Optional reminders and non-judgmental language can make wellbeing interactions more acceptable.
- Concerns about workplace monitoring reinforce the need for transparency and user control.
- Wellbeing support should remain separate from productivity metrics and performance evaluation.

The findings also suggest that AI cannot replace a healthy organizational culture.
